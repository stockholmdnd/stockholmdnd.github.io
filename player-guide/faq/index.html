General clarifications

General Clarifications#

We understand that some rules and rules interactions can be confusing, we ask that you first look to the Sage Advice-compendium, where you will find the official rulings for D&D 5e and 5.5e: https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/sae

Do we follow Rules-As-Written (RAW)?#

Yes, in general, there are two exceptions: Alignment and Lifestyle expenses. We follow everything else that is official in the rulebooks.
Note: Each DM has their own style and can apply any house-rules they want.

Can I have more than one character?#

Yes, you may have as many as you want, but they all start at level 3.

I don’t know any D&D lore, do I have to learn it to play?#

No, your character can be “setting-agnostic”, meaning you don’t have to know the name of your hometown nor all the gods in Faerûn to play. The same goes for the Guild, there is no lore for you to learn, just join a game and play!

Can I buy or craft a magical item?#

Our rules in Stockholm D&D do not allow you to purchase or craft a magical item. However, you do gain access to magical items at certain levels as specified above.

What is the Magical Armor and Firearm-rule?#

When you gain access to a magical item as specified in Permanent Magic Items and choose an item in the Armor-category, or a Firearm in the Weapons-category, you must first own a mundane version of the item.

Can I have a bastion?#

Given The Guild’s structure it is not possible for its members to have individual bastions. Nor do we have a bastion for the Guild.

Can I make an evil character?#

We do not use alignment in Stockholm D&D. You can make an evil-ish character, but remember that you are employed by The Guild, and PvP (Player vs Player) is not allowed. The spirit of the game is that we are adventuring together, and if you make a character that violates that spirit a Dungeon Master can say no. You are responsible for your character, and how they influence the table.

Note: Stockholm D&D reserves the right to take any measures necessary against foul play.

I want to play more often, but I’m not a Dungeon Master. Can I host a game?#

Yes, you can host a game at your home or other location, note that the Dungeon Master must post in #floating-sessions about it.

What happens if my character dies?#

If your character has a life insurance policy and the body is returned from the adventure it will be brought back to life pursuant to the life insurance policy.
If the character cannot be revived it will be buried in our hall of heroes. The Guild will claim any items and gold your character had, i.e. it does not carry over to another character. We therefore expect all Guild-members to return the body and all the possessions of their fallen allies without stealing anything for themselves.

How many spells does a level 3 Wizard start with?#

A starting level 3 Wizard has 10 spells in their spellbook, but remember that you do not have all 10 spells prepared. A maximum of 2 of these spells may be of 2nd level.

The rules for spell-components are confusing to me. What are they?#

Here’s a description of each component, how the cost works, and what “free hand” means.

Verbal#

You must be able to speak to cast the spell. RAW it must be spoken in a normal speaking voice. The words spoken need not be intelligible; it is the pitch, cadence, and resonance that power the spell.

Somatic#

You must have a free hand to make the gestures required to cast the spell.
If the spell has a Material component, you can use one hand for both.
A spell with no Material component requires a free hand.

Free hand#

A spellcaster must know which spells they can cast with what weapon/shield combinations. Here are some clarifications:
Two-handed weapons require two hands to attack, but only one hand to hold. If you use a two-handed weapon, you can still use one hand for Somatic &/or Material.
A shield with a holy symbol can only be used for Somatic if the spell requires a Material component.
If your weapon is a spellcasting focus, it can only be used for Somatic if the spell requires a Material component. You can use “interact with object” on your turn to draw or sheath your weapon, if you want to be able to cast spells and wield a weapon. Keep in mind that if you sheath your sword to cast a spell, you cannot use your sword for an opportunity attack.

Material#

You must have a free hand to handle a component pouch or spellcasting focus to cast the spell.
If the spell requires a specific component with a gold cost or consumes the component, read the rules below.
Here are some common examples of how a spellcasting focus can be used:
A holy symbol can be embedded in your shield.
A staff can also be a quarterstaff.
Certain spellcasting foci are very specific, this is usually a class feature. I.e. Bards may use an instrument as a focus for their Bard spells, but not other classes spells.
Booming Blade has the material requirement “A weapon”, in cases like these you do not need to touch your component pouch or arcane focus.
A component pouch, spellcasting focus, or otherwise specified material must be in hand, to cast the spell.

Gold cost materials#

For any spell that requires a Material component with a specified gold cost, you must have purchased the item and have it in your hand to cast the spell.
I.e. Chromatic Orb requires a 50 gp diamond. It is not consumed so you can use the same diamond every time.
A spell that specifies that the material is consumed costs money every time you cast it. Remember that you need to have bought the components to be able to cast the spell.
I.e. To cast Revivify you need 300gp worth of diamonds every time you cast it. Since the diamonds are consumed you will need to purchase more to be able to cast the spell again.
